Synthetic battery-% tracking line — reconstructed within each lap from the telemetry samples: it drops as energy is deployed (full throttle) and rises as it is harvested (braking / lift-off). The battery starts the race FULL (100% = the 4 MJ usable Energy Store, so 1% = 0.04 MJ) and the opening laps burn it down into the working band — a full store cannot accept regeneration. The dashed 30% / 80% guides are a soft band, not a barrier: steady laps mostly cycle between them, but faster-than-average laps spend stored energy and can dip below 30%, while slower / management laps bank energy and can climb above 80%. Deploy / recover flow is modelled at up to 120 kW = 0.12 MJ/s. Per-lap totals from scripts/energy_simulator.py. The shaded envelope around the line is the SOC uncertainty band — the battery is not broadcast by F1, it is a synthesized estimate (±2% at the simulator's last write, growing ±0.5%/lap, capped ±8%), so the estimate admits its own error instead of showing fake point precision.

Pirelli-style tyre-health model (scripts/tyre_degradation.py): 100% on a fresh set, declining linearly at the per-track measured loss rate — measured from stored race stints (scripts/measure_tyre_wear.py: within-stint lap drift, fuel-corrected, shrunk toward the Pirelli base — Soft ≈ 4.5% · Medium ≈ 2.25% · Hard ≈ 1.35% /lap). Abrasive venues (Bahrain, Barcelona) burn tyres ~1.4× the fleet average; smooth ones (Monaco, Silverstone) ~0.7×. The gold band is the pit window (40–50% health) — teams stop 1–2 laps before the performance cliff below it. The curve ends (gaps) at the 10% extreme-gamble floor — a real stint never sits there, because teams pit before the cliff. Gold diamonds = pit laps — a stop resets the curve. Sources: Pirelli race notes · Flow Racers · F1 Chronicle · measured stints.

One call from both engines: the race projection and the five-policy tactical engine are merged. ⓘ How the merged call decides

One call from both engines. The race projection walks THIS race forward from the current lap — no replay of a finished race: each driver's career (aggregate) pace model predicts their lap time on the current circuit / tyre context, tyre ages tick up one lap at a time, and the gap moves by the modelled pace edge. Once the chaser is within ~1.2 s the per-lap overtake probability is accumulated. The decision engine then rolls five tactical policies (GREEDY ATTACK / BALANCED HOLD / TACTICAL STALK / SAVE & DEFEND / UNDERCUT PREP) through that same simulator and scores them — P(pass) × 6.0 s − 2.0 s/MJ below reserve − tyre wear − P(fail) × 4.0 s − 0.1 s/lap deferral. Both seats always run: the leader engine names the defence it would choose, and that choice sets the posture the chaser engine's attack policies are scored against, so the call must beat the opponent's own recommended answer rather than a hand-set toggle. The seat only decides whose call is surfaced as THE CALL. Any policy (either seat) whose store dips below the reserve target or drains to the 30% floor is INFEASIBLE: pruned, never recommended, listed with its reason. Deterministic — the same state always yields the same call — and the payload reports its own latency. No pit-stop or Safety-Car model: tyres run to the flag, so re-run after stops. Manual per-sector ERS shaping lives in the Energy Lab module below.

ⓘ How the replay works

Uses the leader / chaser / track / year picks in RACE CALL above — resolves the two drivers' real race sessions for that track & season, then replays every shared lap to the flag. Tyre, fuel and ERS (race_state) features are read from the database, so energy_diff is real whenever the energy simulator has run for those sessions. Overtake % is split into S1/S2/S3 by where the chaser is gaining and where the braking evidence sits. Runs again automatically when you re-run the energy simulator for one of these sessions.

ENGINEERING DETAILS — ERS sweep & duel settings

Symmetric sweep re-scores each lap's energy_diff with that mode's projected trace on BOTH cars (only the ERS feature changes, so each row isolates that deployment choice). The asymmetric duel puts the leader on one ERS spec and the chaser on another (adds a LEADER > CHASER row/ring answering when the overtake happens when one attacks while the other defends).

ⓘ What this validates

Reproduces the Trackshift deck's page-8 headline — "1.8s race time improvement" of the AI strategy vs a 'Flat-Out' baseline, 2023 Monaco GP — and extends the same projection to every stored race. Flat-Out = the push mode (full deploy ceiling, battery to the floor, then energy-limited); AI strategy = the fastest mode that finishes above the battery reserve (mode differs per track — balanced on Monaco, liftcoast on high-regen tracks). Closing phase = the final 15 laps; on Monaco 2023 that mean lands at −1.88s (reproduced ✓ vs the deck's −1.8s), while the honest full-race projection is ≈ −9.4s — the deck number is conservative and this panel shows exactly which window produces it. Pace credit = the track's measured s/MJ × deployed MJ (name-alias/case aware, so no track silently falls back to the flat constant); the shared baseline lap time cancels out of every delta. RE-RUN BENCHMARK regenerates the selected race (~seconds, deterministic — same DB yields the same numbers) and REBUILD FLEET SUMMARY re-sweeps all stored races, so the figures can never go stale.
